# Embedding Evaluation

These are comprehensive study notes. Every factual claim is cited. References focus on the standard benchmarks and methodologies used to evaluate the semantic quality of representation spaces.

Any neighborhood-based reliability method relies on the assumption that the embedding space geometrically clusters semantically similar text (The Manifold Hypothesis). If the embeddings are of poor quality, the $k$-Nearest Neighbors will be noise, and the reliability signal will fail. This note covers how we formally evaluate the quality of an embedding space *before* relying on it.

### Conceptual Hierarchy

```text
Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Evaluation
          ↓
  Similarity & Retrieval Benchmarks (BEIR)
          ↓
  The MTEB Standard
```

## 1. Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Evaluation

When an NLP engineer trains a new embedding model (like `Sentence-BERT` or `BGE`), they must prove its representations are meaningful.

### 1.1 Intrinsic Evaluation
Evaluates the geometry of the embeddings directly, without attaching them to a downstream task.
- **Task:** Semantic Textual Similarity (STS).
- **Mechanism:** Given two sentences, humans rate their similarity from 1 to 5. The model computes the Cosine Similarity of their embeddings. The intrinsic score is the Spearman's Rank Correlation between the human scores and the cosine similarities [3].

### 1.2 Extrinsic Evaluation
Evaluates the embeddings by using them as features in a downstream task.
- **Task:** Classification, Clustering, or Retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** Freeze the embeddings, train a linear classifier on top (or run $k$-means), and measure the final F1 score or NDCG.

### 1.3 Comparison: Evaluation Paradigms

| Paradigm | What it Measures | Example Benchmark | Relevance to Reliability Estimation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intrinsic** | The raw geometry (Cosine similarity vs. Human labels). | STS-Benchmark | Ensures $Q_{kNN}$ distances actually represent semantic similarity, not noise. |
| **Extrinsic** | Utility as a frozen feature vector for downstream learning. | SentEval | Ensures the space is sufficiently linearly separable to support Logistic Regression on $JSD$. |

**Why both paradigms matter:**
Geometric reliability methods bridge both. They use the *intrinsic* geometry (distance between neighbors) to predict an *extrinsic* outcome (classification error).

## 2. Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB)

Historically, embeddings were evaluated on scattered, domain-specific datasets. In 2022, Hugging Face and the community established the **MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark)** [1], which is now the absolute gold standard for embedding evaluation.

MTEB evaluates models across 8 distinct task categories:
1. **Bitext Mining:** Finding translations of the same sentence across languages.
2. **Classification:** Standard logistic regression on top of embeddings.
3. **Clustering:** $k$-means clustering, measured via V-Measure.
4. **Pair Classification:** Are two texts duplicates?
5. **Reranking:** Re-ordering search results based on semantic relevance.
6. **Retrieval:** Information Retrieval (IR) on large corpora (incorporates the BEIR benchmark [2]).
7. **STS (Semantic Textual Similarity):** Correlation with human judgment.
8. **Summarization:** Matching summaries to source documents.

## 3. Evaluating Representation Geometry Directly

Beyond MTEB's task scores, we can evaluate the mathematical health of the embedding space directly (as discussed in Note 11).

### 3.1 Alignment and Uniformity
Proposed by Wang and Isola [4], these two metrics perfectly describe an optimal contrastive embedding space:
- **Alignment:** Expected distance between embeddings of positive pairs (should be small).
- **Uniformity:** The logarithm of the expected average distance between all random pairs (should be large, meaning points are uniformly spread across the hypersphere).

Neighborhood-based reliability signals thrive in spaces with high Uniformity. If a space lacks Uniformity, all points collapse into a dense hub, destroying the utility of $Q_{kNN}$.

### 3.2 Measuring Anisotropy
As established by Ethayarajh [5], standard Transformer embeddings are highly anisotropic. This is mathematically evaluated by measuring the proportion of variance explained by the first few Principal Components (PCA). 
- If the first Principal Component explains $90\%$ of the variance, the space is essentially a 1D line, and cosine similarity becomes meaningless.

### 3.3 Distance Concentration
A critical mathematical phenomenon in high-dimensional spaces is **Distance Concentration**. As dimensionality $d \to \infty$ (e.g., $d=768$ or $d=1536$), the relative difference between the distance to the nearest neighbor ($D_{min}$) and the farthest neighbor ($D_{max}$) approaches zero:
$$\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{D_{max} - D_{min}}{D_{min}} \to 0$$
In extreme cases, *everything* looks equidistant. It is therefore essential to mathematically evaluate the variance of pairwise distances within a neighborhood; if variance is near zero, $k$-NN becomes equivalent to random sampling, and $Q_{kNN}$ degenerates into a uniform distribution.

### 3.4 Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID) and Hubness
As extensively covered in Note 11, high-dimensional spaces suffer from **Hubness** (a few points becoming the nearest neighbors to almost everything) and variable **LID**. Evaluating these metrics is a mandatory sanity check.
- **Dense Clusters:** Low LID, high reliability.
- **Boundary / OOD Regions:** High LID, severe Hubness interference, high $JSD$.

## 4. Embedding Drift

Especially relevant for production deployments: representations are not static. The embedding space will evolve after model updates, continual learning, or domain shifts.

```text
Model Retraining → Representation Shift → Invalidated Neighborhoods
```

If the base classifier is updated (even just fine-tuning the classification head), the original feature extraction geometry is altered. The FAISS memory bank cannot remain frozen. 

**The Danger of Drift:**
**Embedding Drift** will cause perfectly valid test points to appear geometrically isolated (high $JSD$) simply because the new model's projection matrix has rotated or translated. If the non-parametric memory is not identically aligned with the current parametric weights, a reliability estimator will output a $100\%$ false positive rate for unreliability.

Production implementations therefore require strict version control locking: a memory bank is only valid for the exact model checkpoint that generated it.
